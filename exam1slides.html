Topic 1: Coulomb's Law

The Foundation

The force between two charges follows an inverse-square law. The key insight is the r2 in the denominator.

F = ke |q1q2| r2

Superposition: Every pair of charges interacts independently. Find each force as a vector (magnitude from formula, direction from signs), then add them.

Common Pitfall: Balance Problems

Students often forget to take the square root when setting forces equal in balance problems. If you reach a step like:

4x2 = 1(3-x)2

STOP. Take the square root of both sides before you cross-multiply. That step is where most algebraic errors occur.

Practice 1 Conceptual

If the distance between two charges is tripled, by what factor does the electric force change?

Answer

The force becomes 1/9 of its original value.

Step-by-Step

Starting from Coulomb's Law, if the new distance is 3r:

Fnew = ke |q1q2| (3r)2 = 19 ke |q1q2| r2 = 19F

Tip: Distance dominates in inverse-square laws. Tripling distance means nine times weaker—not three times.

Practice 2 Exam-Level

Two positive charges lie on the x-axis: +4.0 μC at x=0 and +1.0 μC at x=3.0 m.

Where must a third charge be placed so the net force on it is zero?

Answer

x=2.0 m (between the two charges).

Step-by-Step

The zero-force point lies between them. Let x = distance from the +4 μC charge.

4x2 = 1(3-x)2

Take the square root of both sides before cross-multiplying:

2x = 13-x ⇒ 2(3-x) = x ⇒ 6 = 3x ⇒ x = 2.0 m

Topic 2: Electric Field

The Map of Space

The electric field is the force-per-charge that a source creates in the surrounding space:

E = ke |q|r2

Force from field: F→=qE→

If the test charge is positive, force and field point the same way. If negative, force flips.

Common Misconception

"If the field is zero, there are no charges nearby."

Wrong. Two equal positive charges can cancel each other's fields exactly at their midpoint—charges are very much present. Zero field means vector cancellation, not absence of charge.

Practice 1 Exam-Level

A proton is placed in a uniform electric field E=3.0×104 N/C. Find its acceleration.

Constants: qp=1.60×10-19 C ; mp=1.67×10-27 kg

Answer

a≈2.87×1012 m/s2

Step-by-Step (Two Steps!)

Step 1 - Force: F=qpE=(1.60×10-19)(3.0×104)=4.8×10-15 N

Step 2 - Newton's 2nd Law:

a = Fmp = 4.8×10-15 1.67×10-27 ≈ 2.87×1012 m/s2

Tip: Fields don't just exist—they cause acceleration. Always two steps: field → force → acceleration.

Topic 3: Gauss's Law & Conductors

One Job: Enclosed Charge

Total electric flux through any closed surface equals the net enclosed charge divided by ε0.

ΦE = ∮ E→ · dA→ = Qencε0

The shape and size of the surface do not matter. Only the enclosed charge matters.

Nested Conductors: The One Rule

Apply one rule region by region: the field inside any conducting material is zero.

Draw a Gaussian surface inside the conducting shell's material. Zero field → zero flux → zero enclosed charge.

If the inner object has charge +Q, the shell's inner surface must carry -Q to cancel it. The remaining charge sits on the outer surface. It is bookkeeping—never guessing.

Practice: Shells Exam-Level

An inner solid conducting sphere has charge +Q. It is surrounded by an outer conducting shell that has a net charge of -3Q.

Find the charge on the inner surface and the outer surface of the outer shell.

Answer

Outer shell inner surface: -Q. Outer shell outer surface: -2Q.

Step-by-Step

Step 1: Draw a Gaussian surface entirely within the solid material of the outer shell. Since it's a conductor in equilibrium, E=0 there, so Qenc=0.

Qenc = +Q + Qinner_surface = 0 ⇒ Qinner_surface = -Q

Step 2: The outer shell has a total net charge of -3Q. The total charge is the sum of its surfaces:

Qouter_surface = Qtotal - Qinner_surface = -3Q - (-Q) = -2Q

Topic 5 & 6: Potential & Energy Conservation

Electric Potential

Electric potential is energy per unit charge—a scalar, not a vector. The field points in the direction of decreasing potential (high to low).

Ex = - dVdx

The Most Powerful Application

A charge accelerated through voltage V gains kinetic energy:

|q|V = 12mv2

Energy conservation does all the work without needing the field or the distance!

Practice: Acceleration Standard

An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 200 V. Find its final speed.

Constants: e=1.60×10-19 C ; me=9.11×10-31 kg

Answer

v≈8.39×106 m/s

Step-by-Step

Energy conservation: |q|V=12mev2

v = 2|q|Vme = 2(1.60×10-19)(200) 9.11×10-31 ≈ 8.39×106

Topic 7: Capacitors & Energy Storage

The Most Important Question

Before solving any capacitor problem involving changes (moving plates, adding dielectrics), ask: Is the battery connected or disconnected?

  • Connected: voltage V is fixed, charge adjusts.
    Use U=12CV2.
  • Disconnected: charge Q is fixed, voltage adjusts.
    Use U=Q22C.

Disconnected Case

A charged capacitor is disconnected from a battery. The plate separation then doubles. What happens to the voltage and stored energy?

Voltage doubles. Energy doubles.

Disconnected → Q is constant. Doubling d halves C: Cnew=C/2

Vnew=QC/2=2V

Unew=Q22(C/2)=2U

Connected Case

A capacitor remains connected to a battery while a dielectric (κ=2) is inserted. What happens to C, V, Q, and U?

C doubles, V unchanged, Q doubles, U doubles.

Connected → V is constant. Dielectric: C′=2C

Q′=(2C)V=2Q

U′=12(2C)V2=2U

Exam-Day Summary: Five Rules of Thumb

1. COULOMB Tripling distance → force drops by 9x. Balance problems: set forces equal, take the square root of both sides before cross-multiplying.
2. FIELD Direction first, then magnitude. Force: F→=qE→. Negative charge: force points opposite to the field. Then Newton: a=F/m.
3. GAUSS & SHELLS Only enclosed charge counts. Conductor interior: E→=0. For nested shells, draw a Gaussian surface inside the conductor material where Qenc must be zero.
4. POTENTIAL Field points downhill: Ex=-dV/dx. Speed from voltage is pure energy conservation: |q|V=12mv2.
5. CAPACITORS Ask "connected or disconnected?" first. Connected = V fixed. Disconnected = Q fixed. Dielectric always raises C.
