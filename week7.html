Section 1

The Electromagnetic Spectrum

All electromagnetic waves obey the same physics. What changes is frequency, wavelength, and therefore where they sit on the spectrum.

Big idea: in a vacuum, every electromagnetic wave travels at the same speed, c = fλ. Radio waves, visible light, X-rays, and gamma rays are one family.

Maxwell’s Core Ideas: Induction & Fields

The heart of the unit: changing electric fields create magnetic fields, and changing magnetic fields create electric fields.

Capacitor paradox

The original Ampère’s law works until a charging capacitor forces two valid surfaces to give different enclosed currents.

Original Ampère's Law
∮ B · dl = μ0 Ithrough
SymbolMeaningUnits
BMagnetic fieldT
dlSmall path segment around the loopm

Why it fails for a capacitor: surface S₁ cuts the wire and encloses a real current, but surface S₂ passes through the gap and encloses no conduction current. Same loop, different answers.

Displacement current

A changing electric flux acts like a current for creating a magnetic field.

Equation
Id = ε0 (dΦE / dt)
Id0.2656 A

Faraday’s law

A changing magnetic flux induces an emf and therefore an electric field.

Equation
ℰ = - dΦB / dt
Induced emf1.20 V

Induced field symmetry

Faraday and Ampère-Maxwell are mirror ideas: changing B induces E, and changing E induces B.

Maxwell’s synthesis: changing E-fields create B-fields, and changing B-fields create E-fields. That mutual induction is the mechanism behind electromagnetic waves.
Section 3

Wave Mechanics

Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves: the electric field, magnetic field, and direction of travel are all perpendicular.

Wave geometry

Red = electric field, blue = magnetic field, black = propagation direction.

Speed from Maxwell's equations
c = 1 / √(μ0 ε0)
Field relationship
E = cB
Interpretation: a strong electric field is always accompanied by a magnetic field with magnitude B = E/c. The fields are in phase and locked together.

Section 4

Energy & Radiation Pressure

Light does real work. Electromagnetic waves transport energy, and because they also carry momentum, they can push on matter.

Poynting vector
S = (1/μ0) E × B

Direction of energy flow. The cross product points along the wave’s propagation direction.

Intensity
I = ½ c ε0 E02

Average energy per unit area per unit time. Doubling the field amplitude quadruples the intensity.

Radiation pressure
p = I/c    or    p = 2I/c

Absorbing surfaces get I/c; perfect reflectors get twice that.

Right-hand rule for energy flow

The wave moves in the direction of E × B.

Section 5

Concept Review

Use these as end-of-class checks or embedded review before the final.

Why can’t an EM wave exist with only an electric field?
A traveling electromagnetic wave depends on mutual induction. A changing electric field creates a magnetic field, and a changing magnetic field creates an electric field. If one is missing, the self-sustaining process breaks.
What physical properties determine the speed of light?
The speed of light in vacuum is determined entirely by the electrical and magnetic properties of free space: ε0 and μ0.
If wavelength increases, what must happen to frequency?
It must decrease. Because c = fλ is constant in vacuum, wavelength and frequency are inversely proportional.
Why can EM waves travel through empty space while sound cannot?
Sound needs particles to push and pull on each other. Electromagnetic waves do not need a material medium; the oscillating electric and magnetic fields sustain the disturbance themselves.
