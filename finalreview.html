1. Electric Charge, Coulomb's Law, and Electric Fields

Foundational

Key Equations

$$F_e=k\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}$$

$$\vec E=\frac{\vec F}{q}$$

$$E=k\frac{|q|}{r^2}$$

$k=8.99\times10^9\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$

Need to Know

Electric fields point in the direction a positive test charge would accelerate. Forces between like charges repel; opposite charges attract. For multiple charges, calculate each field or force separately and add as vectors.

Worked Example: Field from a Point Charge

Problem: What is the electric field 0.30 m from a $+4.0\ \mu C$ charge?

$$E=k\frac{|q|}{r^2}=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(4.0\times10^{-6})}{(0.30)^2}$$

$$E\approx4.0\times10^5\ \text{N/C}$$

Direction: away from the positive charge.

2. Electric Flux and Gauss's Law

Key Equations

$$\Phi_E=\int \vec E\cdot d\vec A$$

$$\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A=\frac{Q_{enc}}{\epsilon_0}$$

$\epsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\ \text{C}^2/(\text{N·m}^2)$

Need to Know

Gauss's Law is always true, but it is only easy to use when symmetry lets $E$ come out of the integral. The three big symmetry patterns are spherical, cylindrical, and planar.

SymmetryBest Gaussian SurfaceCommon Result
Point charge / sphereSphere$E=kq/r^2$ outside
Long line of chargeCylinder$E=\lambda/(2\pi\epsilon_0 r)$
Large sheetPillbox$E=\sigma/(2\epsilon_0)$
Concept Check: Why does a conductor have $E=0$ inside?

In electrostatic equilibrium, free charges move until there is no net electric field inside the conducting material. Any remaining internal field would keep pushing charges, meaning equilibrium had not been reached.

3. Electric Potential and Electric Potential Energy

Key Equations

$$V=\frac{U}{q}$$

$$\Delta U=q\Delta V$$

$$V=k\frac{q}{r}$$

$$\Delta V=-\int \vec E\cdot d\vec s$$

Need to Know

Electric field is force per charge. Electric potential is energy per charge. A charge naturally accelerates in a direction that lowers its electric potential energy, not necessarily toward lower voltage unless the charge is positive.

Worked Example: Energy Change

Problem: An electron moves through a potential difference of $120\ \text{V}$. What is the magnitude of its energy change?

$$|\Delta U|=|q\Delta V|=(1.60\times10^{-19})(120)=1.92\times10^{-17}\ \text{J}$$

That is also $120\ \text{eV}$.

4. Capacitance

Trimmed Scope

Key Equations

$$C=\frac{Q}{\Delta V}$$

$$C=\epsilon_0\frac{A}{d}$$

$$U_C=\frac12 C(\Delta V)^2=\frac{Q^2}{2C}=\frac12 Q\Delta V$$

Not on This Review

Series/parallel capacitor network problems are intentionally removed. Focus on what capacitance means, parallel-plate behavior, dielectrics conceptually, and energy storage.

Worked Example: Parallel Plate Capacitor

If plate area doubles, capacitance doubles. If plate separation doubles, capacitance is cut in half. A dielectric increases capacitance by reducing the effective electric field for the same charge.

5. Current, Resistance, Resistivity, and Power

Key Equations

$$I=\frac{dQ}{dt}$$

$$V=IR$$

$$R=\rho\frac{L}{A}$$

$$P=IV=I^2R=\frac{V^2}{R}$$

Need to Know

Current is charge flow rate. Resistance depends on material, length, and cross-sectional area. Power tells how quickly electrical energy is transformed into thermal energy, light, sound, motion, or another form.

Worked Example: Wire Resistance

For a wire, increasing length increases resistance; increasing area decreases resistance. If the same voltage is applied, higher resistance means lower current by $I=V/R$.

6. DC Circuits and Kirchhoff's Rules

Key Equations

Series: $$R_{eq}=R_1+R_2+...$$

Parallel: $$\frac1{R_{eq}}=\frac1{R_1}+\frac1{R_2}+...$$

Junction: $$\sum I_{in}=\sum I_{out}$$

Loop: $$\sum \Delta V=0$$

Need to Know

Series components share current. Parallel components share voltage. Kirchhoff's junction rule comes from charge conservation; loop rule comes from energy conservation.

Exam-Style Table Problem

For each resistor, students should be able to determine resistance, current, voltage drop, and power. The safest process is:

  1. Simplify equivalent resistance.
  2. Find total current from the battery.
  3. Work backward through series/parallel sections.
  4. Use $P=IV$, $P=I^2R$, or $P=V^2/R$ as appropriate.
Kirchhoff Setup: What earns credit?

Even if algebra gets messy, credit comes from clear current labels, correct junction equation, correct signs across resistors and batteries, and loop equations that match the chosen directions.

7. Magnetism

Key Equations

$$F_B=qvB\sin\theta$$

$$F_B=ILB\sin\theta$$

$$r=\frac{mv}{|q|B}$$

$$\tau=NIAB\sin\theta$$

Need to Know

Magnetic forces are perpendicular to motion and field direction. A magnetic field can change direction of velocity but, by itself, does no work on a moving charge because the force is perpendicular to displacement.

Right-Hand Rule Reminder

For positive charges, point fingers in velocity direction, curl toward magnetic field, thumb gives force. For negative charges, reverse the result.

8. Electromagnetic Induction

Key Equations

$$\Phi_B=\int \vec B\cdot d\vec A$$

$$\mathcal{E}=-N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

$$\mathcal{E}=BLv$$

Need to Know

Lenz's Law says the induced current creates a magnetic field that opposes the change in magnetic flux. It opposes the change, not necessarily the original field.

Direction Strategy
  1. Identify the magnetic field through the loop.
  2. Decide whether flux is increasing or decreasing.
  3. Choose the induced field that opposes that change.
  4. Use the right-hand rule to get current direction.

9. Electromagnetic Waves

Capstone Unit

Key Equations

$$I_d=\epsilon_0\frac{d\Phi_E}{dt}$$

$$c=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}}$$

$$E=cB$$

$$I=\frac12c\epsilon_0E_0^2=\frac{E_0B_0}{2\mu_0}$$

$$\vec S=\frac1{\mu_0}\vec E\times\vec B$$

$$c=f\lambda$$

Need to Know

A changing electric field creates a magnetic field, and a changing magnetic field creates an electric field. This mutual regeneration allows electromagnetic waves to travel through empty space.

Maxwell's Fix

Displacement current fixes the capacitor paradox by allowing changing electric flux to act like a current source for magnetic fields.

Wave Geometry

$\vec E$, $\vec B$, and the direction of travel are mutually perpendicular. $\vec E\times\vec B$ points in the direction energy travels.

Energy

The electric and magnetic fields carry equal energy. Intensity scales with amplitude squared.

Spectrum

Radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, x-rays, and gamma rays are the same phenomenon at different wavelengths and frequencies.

Worked Example: Microwave Hotspots

Problem: A microwave oven operates at $f=2.45\ \text{GHz}$. Standing wave hotspots are separated by $\lambda/2$. Find the spacing.

$$\lambda=\frac{c}{f}=\frac{3.00\times10^8}{2.45\times10^9}=0.122\ \text{m}$$

Hotspot spacing: $$\lambda/2=0.061\ \text{m}=6.1\ \text{cm}$$

Worked Example: Fields from Intensity

Problem: An EM wave has $E_0=500\ \text{V/m}$. Find $B_0$ and average intensity.

$$B_0=\frac{E_0}{c}=\frac{500}{3.00\times10^8}=1.67\times10^{-6}\ \text{T}$$

$$I=\frac12c\epsilon_0E_0^2=\frac12(3.00\times10^8)(8.85\times10^{-12})(500)^2$$

$$I\approx332\ \text{W/m}^2$$

10. Special Relativity, General Relativity, and Black Holes

Final Unit

Key Equations

$$\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}$$

Time dilation: $$\Delta t=\gamma\Delta t_0$$

Length contraction: $$L=\frac{L_0}{\gamma}$$

Mass-energy: $$E=mc^2$$

Schwarzschild radius: $$r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2}$$

Need to Know

Special relativity is about motion near light speed. General relativity is about gravity as curved spacetime. Black holes are extreme regions where gravity is so strong that inside the event horizon, light cannot escape.

Special Relativity

Moving clocks run slow relative to an outside observer. Moving lengths contract along the direction of motion.

General Relativity

Mass and energy curve spacetime. Objects follow paths through that curved spacetime.

Black Holes

The event horizon is the boundary beyond which escape requires faster-than-light motion, which is impossible.

Worked Example: Time Dilation

Problem: A spacecraft travels at $0.80c$. If astronauts measure a trip time of 5.0 years, how much time passes for Earth observers?

$$\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-(0.80)^2}}=\frac1{\sqrt{0.36}}=1.67$$

$$\Delta t=\gamma\Delta t_0=(1.67)(5.0)=8.35\ \text{years}$$

Earth observers say the trip takes about 8.4 years.

Worked Example: Length Contraction

Problem: A 100 m ship passes Earth at $0.60c$. What length do Earth observers measure?

$$\gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-(0.60)^2}}=1.25$$

$$L=\frac{L_0}{\gamma}=\frac{100}{1.25}=80\ \text{m}$$

The ship is only contracted along the direction of motion.

Black Hole Concept Check

Near a black hole, time dilation becomes extreme because spacetime curvature is extreme. A distant observer would see clocks near the event horizon run slowly. The falling observer would not personally experience their own clock as running slow.

11. Mixed Final Exam Review

These are designed to feel like final exam preparation without being unfairly brutal. Try each before opening the solution.

Problem 1: Electric Field and Potential

A $+3.0\ \mu C$ charge is placed at the origin. Find the electric field and potential 0.50 m away.

$$E=kq/r^2=(8.99\times10^9)(3.0\times10^{-6})/(0.50)^2=1.08\times10^5\ \text{N/C}$$

$$V=kq/r=(8.99\times10^9)(3.0\times10^{-6})/0.50=5.39\times10^4\ \text{V}$$

Problem 2: Gauss's Law Concept

If a closed surface encloses no net charge, the net electric flux is zero. This does not necessarily mean the electric field is zero everywhere on the surface; field lines may enter and leave in equal amounts.

Problem 3: Circuit Power Table

When given a circuit table, use $V=IR$ first, then compute power using whichever form uses known values: $P=IV$, $P=I^2R$, or $P=V^2/R$. Always check that total power equals battery power.

Problem 4: Kirchhoff Loop

Assign current directions first. At each resistor, moving with current gives $-IR$; moving against current gives $+IR$. Across a battery from negative to positive is $+\mathcal{E}$; positive to negative is $-\mathcal{E}$.

Problem 5: Magnetic Force

For a charge moving perpendicular to a magnetic field, use $F=qvB$. If the charge is negative, the force direction is opposite the right-hand rule result.

Problem 6: Induction Direction

If magnetic flux into the page is increasing, the induced current creates a field out of the page. A counterclockwise current produces an out-of-page field.

Problem 7: EM Wave Intensity

If $E_0$ doubles, intensity quadruples because $I\propto E_0^2$.

Problem 8: Relativity

At higher speed, $\gamma$ increases. That means more time dilation and more length contraction. Relativistic effects become significant only when $v$ is a meaningful fraction of $c$.
