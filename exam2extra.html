Kirchhoff's Laws — Strategy

Fill in each step:

  1. Assume current in each branch.
  2. Apply KCL (Junction): Σ Iin =
  3. Apply KVL (Loop): Σ ΔV around a loop =
  4. Solve the system of equations.

Step 1: Guess current directions — if wrong, the answer comes out negative. That's information, not an error.

Step 2 (KCL): Conservation of charge — what flows in must flow out at every junction.

Step 3 (KVL): Conservation of energy — voltage drops + rises around any closed loop = 0.

Step 4: One equation per unknown current. Solve the system.

With current: ΔV = −IR

Against current: ΔV = +IR

The Two Big Ideas

Moving charges create magnetic fields.
Magnetic fields exert forces on other moving charges.

Field Around a Straight Wire

Fill in the equation:

B = · / (2π · ) B = μ₀I / (2πr)

Why 1/r and not 1/r²?

A long wire is a 1D source — its field spreads over circles of circumference 2πr, so B ~ 1/r. Point sources spread over spheres of area 4πr², giving 1/r².

Right-Hand Rule #1 — Field Around a Wire

  1. Point your right thumb in the direction of the current.
  2. Your curled fingers show the direction B wraps around the wire.
  3. Reverse the current → fingers curl the other way.

Right-Hand Rule #2 — Force on a Moving Charge

F = qv × B

Positive charge: Fingers → v, curl toward B, thumb → F.

Negative charge: Same rule, then reverse the direction of F.

Field at the Center of a Current Loop

B = · / (2 · ) B = μ₀NI / (2R)

N = number of turns, R = radius of the loop (m).

Field Inside a Solenoid

B = · · B = μ₀nI

n = turns per unit length (N/L). Diameter doesn't matter for a long, tightly-wound solenoid — only n and I.

Ampère's Law

∮ B · dℓ = ·

Use Ampère's law when there's enough symmetry that B is constant along the Amperian loop you choose.

Good candidates: long straight wires, solenoids, toroids, coaxial cables.

Torque on a Current Loop

τ = μB sin θ

where μ = NIA is the magnetic moment.

A rotating current loop in a uniform B field experiences torque toward alignment. A commutator reverses the current every half-turn, keeping the torque pushing the same rotational direction.

That's how electrical energy → mechanical rotation.
