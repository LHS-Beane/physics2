Key equations for every topic on the exam. Click a topic to expand.

$$F = k\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}, \qquad k = 8.99\times10^9\,\text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$$

Force is attractive for opposite signs, repulsive for like signs.

Superposition: $\vec F_{\text{net}} = \vec F_1 + \vec F_2 + \cdots$ (vector sum each pair).

$$E = k\frac{|q|}{r^2}$$

Field of a positive charge points outward; negative charge points inward.

$$\vec F = q\vec E \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad a = \frac{qE}{m}$$

Negative charge feels force opposite the field direction.

$$\Phi_E = \oint \vec E \cdot d\vec A = \frac{Q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}, \qquad \varepsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}\,\text{C}^2/(\text{N·m}^2)$$

Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium: $\vec E = 0$.

Outside a spherically symmetric conductor: $E = kQ/r^2$ (same as a point charge).

Charge resides on the outer surface of an isolated conductor.

$$V = k\frac{q}{r} \qquad \text{(point charge, } V=0 \text{ at } \infty)$$

$$\Delta V = \frac{\Delta U}{q}$$

$$E_x = -\frac{dV}{dx} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \text{field points downhill in potential}$$

Potential is a scalar; superpose by algebraic addition.

$$U = k\frac{q_1 q_2}{r} \qquad \text{(two-charge system)}$$

$$\Delta U = q\Delta V \qquad W_{\text{field}} = -\Delta U$$

Acceleration from voltage: $\displaystyle v = \sqrt{\frac{2qV}{m}}$ (energy conservation).

$$C = \varepsilon_0\frac{A}{d} \qquad \text{(parallel plate)}$$

$$Q = CV$$

$$U = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{1}{2}QV$$

With dielectric: $C' = \kappa C$. Fixed voltage → $U$ increases; fixed charge → $U$ increases when $C$ decreases.

$$I = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t} \qquad \text{(current = charge per second, A = C/s)}$$

$$V = IR \qquad \Leftrightarrow \qquad I = \frac{V}{R} \qquad \Leftrightarrow \qquad R = \frac{V}{I}$$

Resistance is a property of the resistor and does not change when voltage changes.

$k = 8.99\times10^9\,\text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$

$\varepsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}\,\text{C}^2/(\text{N·m}^2)$

$e = 1.60\times10^{-19}\,\text{C}$

$m_e = 9.11\times10^{-31}\,\text{kg}$

$m_p = 1.67\times10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$

$1\,\mu\text{C} = 10^{-6}\,\text{C}$

$1\,\text{nC} = 10^{-9}\,\text{C}$

$1\,\text{V} = 1\,\text{J/C}$
